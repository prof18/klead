Andrew Bulkeley
May 15, 2026

Dear 20 Percent,

I bumped into a report about how real estate developers have become so annoyed with the glacial pace of Berlin bureaucrats that they’ve just started doing work, deciding to worry about permits when bureaucrats get around to it.

The report included a construction site on the westside that blocked off traffic lanes, set up scaffolding and even brought in one of those towering construction cranes — all without permission and without officials even being aware of it.

Fake it till you make it or set up for the permit you want, not the one you don’t have, or something.

This is really the spirit that created modern Berlin — do whatever you want. Nobody is watching — even the agencies who are supposed to be. I just can’t believe it’s still around.

Have a good, supervision-free weekend! Weather is looking good!

Andrew

Two-week limit three years too late

Everyone who needed an appointment with Bürgerämter in March was able to get one within two weeks, according to RBB24, finally meeting a goal Mayor Kai Wegner (CDU) had promised for 2023. Many were even able to get same- or next-day appointments. Appointments had been so hard to come by early in this decade that unethical entrepreneurs had begun using bots to snap up and sell them. But officials have worked to move a few procedures online and hire 100 new bureaucrats to process the often-repetitive and too-often unnecessary paperwork. Now make the Ausländerbehörde more accessible (and friendly).

Pols hate cash-only too

Berlin politicians are tired of cash-only businesses and want the federal government to force retailers to accept at least one form of digitial payment, according to RBB24. A parliamentary committee Wednesday agreed to pressure the Bundesrat, one of Germany’s two parliaments, in-line with recent noise made by federal politicians in an attempt to dampen tax avoidance and make life easier for us consumers. Germany loses out on an estimated €2 billion in annual sales tax revenue while I am often forced to opt for major retailers and fast-food joints because of the short-sighted practice of accepting only cash by local merchants. Only the local 💩AfD opposed the measure.

Gun crime raids

Berlin and federal cops Wednesday raided 28 addresses looking for weapons used in recent crimes, according to Tagesspiegel. 570 officers — including the elite GSG9 federal SWAT team — arrested nine suspects and confiscated drugs, cash, two cars and a single weapon. 570 officers. 1 gun. 🤔 Police and prosecutors have had little luck in controlling an explosion in gun-related crime, which leapt 68% last year. The near-daily shootings rarely result in serious injury and are often attributed to Turkish and Kurdish gangs attempting to establish dominance while blackmailing businesses into protection payments.

Tschüss Eta!

The first-ever female head coach of a Bundesliga mens soccer team will coach her last match Saturday, according to RBB24. Marie-Louise Eta will then become head of 1. FC Union Berlin’s womens team after making history April 3 by being appointed to the leadership position. She was not only the first female coach of a Bundesliga mens team but also the first woman to coach a men’s football team in the the top-five European leagues. Union, Berlin’s eastside club, failed to blossom under her leadership. However, she was able to save it from relegation to a lower league. Union has yet to announce a replacement.

Factoid

Berliner’s throw away more per-person than the average German or an average 231.6 kgs per Berliner per year compared with just 186.7 kgs per Kraut nationwide, according to the Berlin-Brandenburg statistics office. The figures are for 2024, the first year that people threw away more than the previous year since 2020. The figure includes both ordinary household trash and Sperrmüll, or bulky items like that refrigerator or mattress abandoned on your street corner.
