In DepthA bitter battle for control is shaking the beverage giant. The spiritual and financial legacy of its charismatic founder, Pierre Castel, who has just turned 100 and is France's 11th-richest person, is a stake.

![PARIS, FRANCE - MAY 31: CEO of Castel Group Gregory Clerc on May 31, 2024 in Paris, France. (Photo by Manuel Braun/Contour by Getty Images)](https://img.lemde.fr/2026/11/20/0/0/2625/3500/2301/0/75/0/4f5c077_upload-1-wly4lqpugvxj-gettyimages-2187492495.jpg)

A small gathering of relatives and close associates raised a glass on Sunday, October 17, at the home in southern Portugal where Pierre Castel, the now-frail founder of the Castel Group, has retired. There were, of course, balloons, a birthday cake topped with candles and even a barrel specially engraved with "Pierre Castel, 100." In front of the buffet set up in the garden to enjoy the sunshine despite the wind, about 20 guests gathered around the man who built an empire of beer and wine. Among them were Romy Castel, his only daughter, and Alain and Philippe Castel, two of his nephews. Letters were read aloud. "It was very moving. Even though he is no longer involved with the group, people are very grateful," said Romy, 52, in a rare interview with *Le Monde* at the end of October. "He cried a lot – in a good way, not a bad way."

For the past year, an unprecedented conflict has shaken the group that Pierre led with an iron fist throughout his life. A bitter and internal battle has played out across three continents, involving legal proceedings, personal attacks and legions of advisers, lawyers and communications experts. "Even bankers no longer know whom to trust. It's statement against statement. The two sides seem to be living on two different planets," summed up a person with close knowledge of the group.
