By Phil Hay

Hello! We’re back with a special edition of The Athletic FC newsletter. Those financial charges brought against Manchester City by the Premier League? The verdict is in…

Breaking News

 

Dan Mullan/Getty Images

Man City found guilty on almost all EPL charges relating to financial breaches

For what felt like an eternity, football at large had been waiting for the outcome of Manchester City’s alleged breaches of the Premier League’s financial rules. It’s no longer waiting because the news has dropped: City are guilty on all but one of the 115 charges against them. Our live blog is up and running with all the very latest.

 

We should temper expectations of the details at the outset because sanctions are still to be decided. The Premier League hasn’t publicly confirmed the story broken by The Athletic’s David Ornstein, but the implications of the verdict for City are likely to be highly damaging. Numerous forms of punishment are open to the Premier League: a financial penalty, a points deduction, and even the possibility of expulsion from the competition. That’s without touching on the reputational impact.

 

How did we get here?

City were charged in 2023 with multiple breaches of the financial regulations set by the Premier League. The alleged offences occurred between 2009 and 2018 and were first highlighted by reports in German outlet Der Spiegel.

 

The case was sent to an independent commission to adjudicate, with City vehemently protesting their innocence. The wait for a verdict has been excruciatingly long, but according to David’s sources, the commission has ruled that City are guilty of 114 of the 115 allegations against them. It’s not yet known on which count they were cleared.

 

It hardly needs pointing out that we’re talking about one of the biggest, wealthiest and most dominant clubs in the world. Over the past decade and a half, City — under Abu Dhabi ownership — have won eight Premier League titles, the first three of which were claimed in the period covering their financial breaches. They won the Champions League for the first time in 2023, and hired one of the game’s most exceptional coaches, Pep Guardiola, in 2016.

 

What have City said?

The only formal response to David’s report so far has come from a spokesperson at City, who have always denied the allegations. They said: “The Premier League process is ongoing, with significant elements to be completed, and subject to strict confidentiality. As such, Manchester City FC’s position remains consistent with the club’s statement of February 2023 (NB: at the time, City described themselves as “surprised” by the charges).

 

“The club has diligently respected due process for eight years on the basis that the Premier League board and executive would behave as an independent, impartial and fair-minded regulator, free from partisan influence.”

 

What happened in the interim?

Ed Garvey/Manchester City FC via Getty Images)

It’s probably helpful to briefly go back over some of the key facts and figures at City in the period their charges covered, from 2009 to 2018. And also to show how far they advanced competitively:

  • Four permanent managers were employed in that timeframe: Mark Hughes, Roberto Mancini, Manuel Pellegrini and Guardiola, who took the reins in July 2016.

  • Centre-back Aymeric Laporte was their most expensive signing, costing £57m from Athletic Club in Spain. Kevin De Bruyne (£55m from German side Wolfsburg) had broken City’s club record two-and-a-half years earlier.

  • They won three Premier League titles, the first in 2011-12 on the occasion of the famous Sergio Aguero goal (above). They claimed an FA Cup, three League Cups and a Community Shield. 

  • Their maiden Champions League appearance came in 2012, and they reached the semi-finals for the first time in 2016. Those were key steps towards lifting the trophy in 2023.
 

What don't we know yet?

Today’s news isn’t the end of this process by any means, and the complexity of it has been shown by the time taken for a verdict to drop. The independent commission heard evidence over 12 weeks in 2024 and City's lawyers will now look at options open to them to appeal. The Premier League has been under intense and increasing pressure to bring the matter to a conclusion.
